Narrow Library Bedroom

Working drawing set. Twin bed spanning the full width, stepped floor-to-ceiling book wall, windowless room.

Room 13′-8″ × 6′-4″ × 9′-0″
Bed Twin, 38″ × 75″
Shelving 18″ base / 12″ upper
Daylight None
Units Inches

Sheet 1 · Floor Plan

Bed spans wall to wall at the far end. The shelf run starts flush with the bed’s foot edge and runs the full 126″ to the near wall, uninterrupted because the door swings out.

  • Mattress is 75″ long in a 76″ opening. That 1″ of total slack rules out any conventional frame and is why the plinth is built to the room.
  • Shelf run begins at y = 38″ from the far wall, flush with the bed’s foot edge. Its 18″ projection is what screens the pillow end from the doorway.
  • Confirm hallway clearance for the outward swing and that the leaf does not collide with an opposing door.

Sheet 2 · Left Wall Elevation

The stepped run, seen from the room. Closed base to 32″, ledge, then open book shelving to the ceiling. Roughly 63 linear feet of shelf on this wall alone.

  • Vertical gaps run tallest at the bottom (14″) and tighten going up (10″ at the top), so oversized art books live where you can reach them.
  • Top shelf at 98″ needs a step stool. Treat it as deep storage rather than daily reach.
  • Closed doors at the base rather than open baskets: closed fronts bounce light back and keep the floor line quiet in a room with no daylight.

Sheet 3 · Far Wall Elevation

Two shelves above the bed, both clearing the head-strike line. The lower shelf is deliberately shallow so the wall does not loom over the pillow.

  • Mattress top sits at 22″. A seated adult measures 34–38″ from seat to crown, so heads top out near 56–60″. Shelf A’s underside at 62″ clears that with buffer.
  • The left wall above the pillows stays completely clear. That is the surface people genuinely sit up into, and a shelf there would be a hazard. All bed lighting comes from under Shelf A, so nothing projects.
  • Shelf A is a comfortable overhead reach from seated in bed. Shelf B is not. Put nothing you need nightly on B.

Sheet 4 · Section Through the Shelf Run

The step at larger scale. This detail is the whole design: depth sits low, below your sightline, so the room still measures 64″ wide where your eye actually travels.

  • The whole scheme hangs on this step. Floor clearance drops to 58″, but at standing eye level the room still reads 64″ wide, and the eye never travels to the floor.
  • LED channels shown routed into the shelf fronts and the ledge. On an IKEA-based build these become surface-mounted channels instead, which is the main visual compromise of that route.
  • Toe kick recessed 3″ so you can stand close to the shelves without your toes catching.

Sheet 5 · Reflected Ceiling & Lighting Plan

No daylight reaches this room, so light is the architecture. The existing central fixture becomes a junction point rather than the main source.

  • Why the central fixture gets demoted. One source in the middle of a 6′4″ room throws matching shadows down both long walls, flattens the book spines, and pulls the eye straight to the narrow dimension. Its value here is the live feed it provides.
  • Why the wall wash matters most. A brightly lit long wall visually recedes. Turning the full 13′8″ × 9′ bare plane into a soft light source widens the room more than any change to the floor plan could.
  • Avoid a centre line of downlights. It would spotlight exactly the proportion you want people to stop noticing.

  • Code and egress. In much of the US a room without an egress window cannot legally be designated a bedroom. It does not change how well the room works, but it can affect appraisal, insurance and resale. Confirm locally before commissioning joinery.
  • Ventilation. A sealed interior room used for sleeping needs real air exchange. Confirm supply and return, or plan a transfer grille or inline fan.
  • Construction route. The plinth is custom either way. For the run, a hybrid is likely best value: stock carcasses for the four upper book bays, custom work for the base, the ledge and the plinth. Verify actual stock depths first, since common bookcase depths sit close to but not exactly at 12″.
Drawings are dimensioned in inches and drawn to scale. Verify all dimensions on site before fabrication, particularly the 76″ width at floor, mattress height and ceiling, since the 1″ bed tolerance leaves no margin for out-of-square walls.
